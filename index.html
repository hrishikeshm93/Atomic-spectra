Part 1

Electron orbits

12.2.2 The Rutherford picture

Rutherford’s nuclear model uses classical physics. The atom is an electrically neutral sphere. At its centre sits a very small, massive, positively charged nucleus. Electrons revolve around it in their own dynamically stable orbits.

  • The Coulomb force of attraction Fe between electron and nucleus acts toward the nucleus.
  • This force supplies the required centripetal force Fc.
  • So for a stable orbit: Fe = Fc.

Step-by-step derivation

Tap Next step to build the result one line at a time. The electron has charge −e and mass m; it moves at speed v in a circle of radius r (hydrogen: nucleus charge +e).

Step 1 · Force of attraction (Coulomb’s law)

Fe = 14πε0e²r²

Step 2 · Centripetal force needed for circular motion

Fc = mv²r

Step 3 · Dynamically stable orbit: Fe = Fc

14πε0e²r² = mv²rEq. 12.2

Step 4 · Relation between orbit radius and speed

Rearrange 12.2 for r:

r = e²4πε0mv²Eq. 12.3

Step 5 · Kinetic energy

Multiply 12.2 by r/2 so that ½mv² appears on the right:

K = ½mv² = e²8πε0r

Step 6 · Potential energy

Electrostatic PE of the electron–nucleus pair (zero at infinity). The negative sign says the force points along −r, toward the nucleus:

U = − e²4πε0r

Step 7 · Total energy E = K + U

E = e²8πε0r − e²4πε0r = − e²8πε0rEq. 12.4

Step 8 · What the minus sign means

E is negative, so the electron is bound to the nucleus. If E were positive, the electron would not follow a closed orbit. Also note: U = −2K and E = −K.
Think: An accelerating charge should radiate energy and spiral into the nucleus, yet atoms are stable. Classical physics cannot explain this. The line spectra in Part 2 give the clue that leads to Bohr’s model.

Part 2

Atomic spectra

12.3 What is an atomic spectrum?

  • Every element has a characteristic spectrum of radiation that it emits.
  • When an atomic gas or vapour at low pressure is excited, usually by passing an electric current through it, the emitted light has only specific wavelengths.
  • This is the emission line spectrum: bright lines on a dark background.
  • Because each element has its own set of lines, the spectrum works like a fingerprint to identify the gas.
  • Pass white light through a gas and analyse the transmitted light: dark lines appear at exactly the wavelengths the gas emits. This is the absorption spectrum.
Spectrum = light split by wavelength (using a prism or grating in a spectrometer). The pattern tells us what the source is made of.

Key takeaways

  • For a stable orbit, Coulomb force = centripetal force: e²/(4πε₀r²) = mv²/r.
  • K = e²/(8πε₀r), U = −e²/(4πε₀r), E = −e²/(8πε₀r) < 0, so the electron is bound.
  • Atoms emit only specific wavelengths: the emission line spectrum, a fingerprint of the element.
  • Absorption dark lines sit at the same wavelengths as the emission lines.
  • Hydrogen lines form series: Lyman (UV), Balmer (visible), Paschen (infrared).
